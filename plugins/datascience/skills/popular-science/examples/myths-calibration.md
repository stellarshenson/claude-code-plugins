# Calibration exemplar - the "myths" register

Target density for a debunking passage: name the myth, break it with a NAMED source and a HARD number, close on a witty turn. A claim alone is weak; the research team and the numbers sell the story. Each item below carries all three - the claim, its provenance or its number, and a last line that lands.

Mirror this shape, not this topic.

---

The myth of a return to tradition - that reviving the male-breadwinner, stay-at-home-mother family would refill the nurseries. The data say the reverse: the societies with the most traditional division of housework - Korea and Japan foremost - sit at the very bottom of the world's fertility table, while the more equal Nordic homes sit well above them. Ask a woman to carry a full job and the entire household, and many will decline the second child rather than the career. In the model a birth in a low-fertility country happens roughly only when both partners want it (Doepke & Kindermann, 2019), and a woman's own earnings flip from fertility-suppressing to fertility-raising precisely when men share the load at home. The lever is fairness, not nostalgia.

The myth of locking the exits - that making divorce harder, or penalising people for keeping their options open, would hold families together and lift births. Raising the cost of leaving removes the very bargaining that lets couples de-escalate; where divorce was restricted, women's suicide and domestic violence rose (Stevenson & Wolfers, 2006), and in the model lock-in levers backfire. The exit is not the leak - it is the valve.

The myth of the baby bonus - that a big enough cheque will do it. Cash buys a brief flurry of births, mostly from couples who were having the child anyway and merely bring it forward, and then the effect evaporates. Korea spent on the order of USD 270 billion over twenty years and watched its birth rate fall in every one of them.

The myth that people simply stopped wanting children - a genuine change of heart exists, but the model finds it the smallest of the three causes. The largest by far is that fewer people are pairing into the stable couples children mostly come from (Sobotka, 2017); the "they just don't want them" story mistakes the smallest cause for the whole.

The myth of the permanent immigration fix - immigration is the one lever that pays off inside a single lifetime, and it holds some countries level today. But it reshuffles the world's existing young; it neither explains nor repairs why a country stopped producing its own, and the planet as a whole cannot immigrate its way out.

---

## Why this works
- **Claim + provenance + number** - each myth is broken with a named study (Doepke & Kindermann 2019, Stevenson & Wolfers 2006, Sobotka 2017) or a hard figure (USD 270 billion over 20 years). A bare "cash doesn't work" would be half-believed; the number makes it seen
- **The witty turn** - each closes on a line that reframes ("The exit is not the leak - it is the valve"), the kicker at paragraph scale
- **Parallel structure** - "The myth of…" repeated builds rhythm and lets the reader scan the set
- **Honest magnitude** - "the smallest of the three causes", "the largest by far" - direction and size stated, never fuzzed into an adjective
